# Mira — Journey Part 2

Told by Shu, a child raised by a drunken raider uncle who uses him (and his cursed family blade, Yin Qi) as a weapon. Shu saves a baby from a raid and plans to flee with her and his cousin [[Tray]]; when the uncle discovers the plan, a young wind-wielding woman — the "girl" of this story, i.e. Mira per the story title — frees them, takes them as her first crew, and flies them to Blackwater ("Home"). The baby is named Ju Lee after Shu's mother (cf. [[Ju Lee Caneheart]]).

---


A village in the East Blue.

The blood got soaked into the blade as the woman extended her fingertips with her last breath toward a room. I sheathed my blade as the screams faded.

“May yelled, ‘Hey rat, we are gonna leave you behind. You coming or what?’”

I turned to go out of the burning hut... but my feet stopped when I heard the cry.

I walked out of the hut carrying a baby still crying.

Jay slapped my head.

“The hell did you bring this time? You think Dad's gonna let you take that? You should be grateful that he took a rat like you in to begin with. Just kill it like the rest and carry that passed-out pretty face in with you, once you're done carrying all the valuables onto the ship.”

May, as usual, looked at me with disgust as she walked to the ship.

I disgusted myself, soaked in blood from head to toe.

I put the baby on the ground as she wailed until the last bit of the burning hut crumbled. I carried Tray in and came back to see Uncle holding the baby.

“Ahh! So this is the thing that's been waking me up all night,” he said, still chugging the bottle he stole from the last village.

He threw the bottle on the ground as he pulled out his blade.

My heart sank as I ran and grabbed his hand.

“Please, Uncle! Just let me take care of her. I promise she won't cause any more problems. You can beat me if you want to.”

He kicked me as I fell to the ground.

“You think I enjoy beating you, huh? I only do it 'cus it's my duty to discipline you. Why are you such a troublemaker? Now you want me to feed this thing too? You know how hard it is for a child to survive on the sea?”

He drunkenly threw the baby toward me as I jumped to catch her.

“I'm sorry, Uncle. I won't ask for more food. The bread you give me each day is enough?”

“Damn right it's enough. I need to feed the men too, y'know. I would have given you more if we could afford it,” he said as he looked down on me with the glare in his narrow, drunken eyes.

He wiped his thin mustache as he heavily stamped his foot. The splash of mud sprayed onto my face.

I took this as a small victory.

I was used to my life ever since my uncle took me in to teach me the ways of the world. My grandfather had fallen ill and had written in his will to ask my uncle to take me with him with a hefty sum of Beli.

But the family sword was handed down to me.

The cursed blade, Yin Qi. Only the owner of the blade can transfer its ownership.

My Uncle always hated me for that.

He used me as a weapon and pointed me in the direction of his enemies. I had no choice but to obey, or he'd just make his men torture me... or worse, he'd threaten to hurt Tray.

He was the only friend I had. The only friend I ever knew.

Unlike Jay and May, Tray was weak and cowardly according to some.

To me, he was the bravest one.

He was the only one of Uncle's children who ever stood up for me.

My uncle did not like Tray. He was a bastard from a witch he met in the Grand Line. Jay and May didn't treat him well either, but they never pushed him enough to make him angry.

Not a lot made him angry to begin with. He would listen to their taunts, even laugh with them at the jokes made at his expense.

I had only heard whispers about it, but the crew used to whisper about an incident from before I met my uncle.

They said Tray nearly killed him once.

I used to believe they were just stories they made up while drunk.

He was my light.

But now I had one more, and I couldn't stand to think about the life she would have if she stayed here.

The next year went by quickly.

At first, she would cry for hours. I would steal milk or water or crush whatever fruit I could grab to feed her.

My Uncle would come in while I hid her away. He would take out his frustrations on me.

Most of the men on the ship were mercenaries. They knew better than to interfere with the captain's decisions. Treason was a quick way to a swift death.

They ignored him most of the time. Sometimes a few would step in, tell him he was too drunk, and take him away.

Jay and May would come by from time to time, throwing things at me while I covered her.

But it was all worth it when Tray stopped by, laughing and telling his dumb jokes. He even made the baby laugh once.

After a while, she stopped crying.

Maybe she learned that crying only led to my bruises.

She learned to be quiet just like me.

Like me, I didn't want her life to be like mine.

I decided I would take her away with me during the next raid.

As usual, my uncle told me to go into the village alone and kill every man that looked like they could fight.

Then they could come in and massacre everyone—the old, the women, and the children—then loot their houses, set them on fire, and that would be enough for a couple of weeks of their late-night parties.

I walked in and took a breath, knowing this would be the last time I'd have to be this... a weapon for killing.

I unsheathed the blade, and screams erupted as usual, praying for my end, wishing me nothing but anguish and despair.

I walked to the first man as he waved at me.

I slit his throat.

The rest went as usual.

Denial.

Anger.

Bargaining.

Depression.

Acceptance.

As I killed more and more, I felt myself accepting what I was doing.

Once I was done, I waited until they were ready to leave.

I walked onto the ship.

“Shu... I heard that you wanted to leave us, huh?” my uncle said, turning with the baby in his arms.

“No, Uncle. I wouldn't. Please, just put her down.”

I saw Jay and May walk out from behind him as they threw away all the supplies I had gathered over a month.

The supplies I had gathered to escape.

They stepped on them.

“We will run away... don't you worry. I'll always protect you. Blah blah blah,” Jay said, making faces.

“You are so gross. OMG, just die already,” May said as she laughed.

I had lost all hope.

I bent to my knees as I crawled my way to Uncle's feet.

“Is the fighting over? What is happening? It better be over. I can't see any blood, okay?”

Tray said this as he walked out of the small chest he had locked himself in.

“What are you doing, Dad?”

He looked at the food and supplies he had helped me gather, now crushed and destroyed.

He went quiet.

The atmosphere changed as the crew nervously tried to reason with Tray.

“Oh! This is nothing. We are just playing a game.”

His fist started glowing a bright red as his blue hair flowed in the air.

A large gust of wind filled the sails as the ship jolted forward.

My uncle dropped the baby, and I caught her.

“Tell me... were you the ones who did this?”

A loud, angry voice echoed from the sky as the clouds gathered and the wind clashed against itself.

The ship rocked as whirlpools started forming all the way around it.

“TELL ME! WERE YOU THE ONES WHO KILLED THESE PEOPLE?”

The voice became louder and clearer.

I gazed up at the mast.

A flying woman...

No, a girl.

Tall, but she couldn't be older than May.

She descended through the raging wind, her red hair flowing behind her.

I saw the men tackle Tray and put those big blue cuffs on him.

The rest took up arms and started firing at the figure.

The bullets stopped in the air.

She kept descending, her face twisted with anger, her eyes a piercing green.

We could hear her teeth grinding.

I thought she was a Marine, but I'd heard tales from my grandfather about people called Devil Spawn.

She raised her arms as water started rising all around the ship, the power of the wind somehow funneling it through the whirlpools.

Then she looked down and locked eyes with me.

Her face softened as she gazed at the baby in my arms.

My uncle took this chance to put a gun to my head.

“We... did? maybe some, but he is the one responsible for the downfall of that village. We can come to an agreement. You're a pirate, correct?”

He pointed at the flag tied to one of her arms.

A bearded skull and crossbones.

The water dropped from the air back into the sea.

She gazed at a knocked-out Tray, then looked at the floor as she softly landed on the deck.

The whirlpools stopped.

The sails became calm in the wind.

“You know who I am?”

She looked at me throughout the conversation and just smiled.

“No, but I think I might have gone over into another territory. We are actually leaving for the Grand Line, so we needed some supplies. Unfortunately, we didn't have the Beli to afford reinforcements for our hull.”

He lied through his teeth to the girl. Cowards like him wouldn't survive in the grand line.

“But we are glad to give all of the spoils to you. Where is your ship?”

“I don't have one,” she replied, still looking at me.

This enraged my Uncle as he signaled his men to put those same cuffs on the girl.

As one of the crew members tried to jump her, she moved a hand, and a gust of wind threw him overboard into the sea.

Soon, he disappeared from sight.

Her expression changed as she looked up at Uncle.

“That wasn't very smart.”

All the men, maybe because of fear or maybe because they had always been insane to begin with, tried to move against the girl.

She moved her finger in the air as air pressure gathered around it.

She waved her hand.

The pressurized wind pierced the men's chests as they all fell to the ground.

“You all deserve to die like scum. Be glad I didn't take you to the captain.”

My uncle reached down and snatched the baby.

He whispered, “Kill her, and you and this thing can have your freedom.”

After what I had witnessed, I had little hope of surviving.

But the only thing on my mind was to get her and get out.

I drew my blade as the voices in my head grew louder.

I jumped at the girl with my blade drawn.

She grabbed the blade with her bare hands as her blood soaked into the blade.

This was the first time I actually saw a memory.

*A large figure holding the hand of a small and fragile girl basking in the desert sun* 

The warmth spread through my body

“The voices... they're gone. How?”

She took the sword away, gently pressed her hand against my chest, and I fainted.

I opened my eyes to see my Uncle with swollen eyes and blood running down his face.

Jay and May were tied together with him, in the center of the deck while I lay beside Tray, now out of his cuffs but sound asleep.

I panicked as I looked for the baby.

All I heard were giggles.

A sound I hadn't heard in a while.

I looked up and saw the girl playing with the baby as she flew her around like a bird.

Without even turning, as if she knew I was looking at them, she asked,

“What's her name?”

“Sorry?”

“What's her name?”

She turned as she floated down.

“She doesn't have one,” I replied as she handed the baby to me.

“Well, that's a shame... Look, kid, I'm gonna go to the village to pay respects to the dead. It's your decision what you do with them... meet me down there once you're done.”

My eyes gazed at my “family.”

Then I looked back at Tray.

“I can't. It's his family too. I can't take it away from him.”

“The blood of the covenant is thicker than the water of the womb. It's an old saying someone told me once. It's your decision. It's neither wrong nor right.”

“What makes you think I'm gonna follow you? How do I know you won't just use me like him?”

She bent down to take another look at the baby.

I whispered, “Ju Lee,” to myself as I gazed at the baby's eyes.

“That's a beautiful name... Who was she? I know you only said the name because of a reason,” she inquired as she stretched and got ready to walk out.

“It was my mother's name. My grandfather told me once.”

“I see. To answer your question...”

She turned and smiled back before flying off.

“You are of no particular use to me... I just thought you needed a friend.”

I walked down after a few minutes, carrying Tray with me.

I never told her what I did. I did tell Tray once I thought he was ready to hear it.

But I imagine she already knew.

I sat next to her and joined her in the prayers.

The tears started to flow. They couldn't stop.

I tried to push them back with my hands, wiping them away.

I felt her hand reach for my head as she hugged me.

“It's okay... You can let it out. A kid is supposed to cry...” she said softly.

Ju Lee started crying as well.

She moved her hand through my sticky, muddy hair.

“If I ever tell you to use that blade, that's the day you'll know I'm not the same person who met you today.”

A few moments passed as we all calmed down.

“Alright, gang... we ready to go. Give me an Aye Aye, Cap'n!”

I wiped my tears as I said,

“Aye Aye, Cap'n.”

With a sparkle in her eyes, she turned around and started mumbling to herself.

“Maybe I can finally get my own crew now... Hehe. Old man, you really thought I couldn't find people to follow me, huh? Well, who's laughing now... hehehe.”

I could hear her, but pretended not to.

She lifted all of us into the air with her winds.

“Where are we going?” I asked.

She turned around to the morning sun.

“Blackwater... Home.”

As we all flew, piercing through the air.
